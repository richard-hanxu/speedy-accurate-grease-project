# News-provenance batch 4: Strait of Hormuz ship attacks, 6–11 Oct 2026

## Method

- **X/Twitter post IDs were decoded to exact UTC times** using the snowflake formula `((id >> 22) + 1288834974657) ms`. These are marked **[snowflake]** and are exact to the second.
- **UKMTO warning PDFs** (`ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/2026MMDD-ukmto_warning_NNN-26.pdf`) and **JMIC Advisory Notes 103, 104 and 105** were downloaded with curl and read directly. The JMIC notes map warning numbers to vessel names.
- **Article timestamps** come from page metadata (`datePublished`, for example Seatrade) or the time shown on the page. Where I converted a time zone, I say so.
- **ukmto.org and seatrade blocked WebFetch, but curl worked.** Vanguard Tech posts (the most common source for vessel names) were not directly retrievable, so their timing is inferred from the first article citing them.

## Per-event notes

**1. On Peace**
- India's foreign ministry (MEA) was the first public source and also named the ship. Its X post went out at 10:15Z on 6 Oct.
- UKMTO's warning 157-26 was a "time-late" report. JMIC says its source was the MEA itself, so UKMTO lagged a government statement.
- Oman's defence ministry posted about seven hours later, about the airlift of 10 crew to Khasab.
- **Date conflict:** JMIC and UKMTO put the incident on 5 Oct; the MEA said "earlier today" (6 Oct).
- gCaptain linked On Peace to UKMTO 156-26 (16:37Z, 5 Oct). JMIC 103 shows 156-26 was an LNG carrier, so that link is wrong.

**2. Acers**
- Textbook UKMTO-first case: incident at 1900Z, UKMTO X post 40 minutes later, AFP wire within about 25 more minutes.
- The vessel name came from Vanguard Tech roughly 6 hours later; Seatrade had it by 01:48Z.
- JMIC 103 later revised the details: 3 UAVs, an engine-room fire, 1 injury.

**3. Gem No. 2**
- Vanguard (strike time 1000Z) and a security company that reported to UKMTO at 11:34Z were upstream of UKMTO.
- Trade press (Seatrade) had the name about 4 hours after the strike. The Reuters wire followed about 45 minutes later and relied on "two shipping and security sources" for the name.
- The UAE government statement was vague and late (evening).

**4. Valthera**
- The UKMTO post came about 2h50m after the master's report.
- Indian officials dated the attack to 11 Oct; UKMTO and Vanguard dated it to 10 Oct. Treat 10 Oct, about 19:20Z, as the incident time.
- The IRGC "supertanker mine" claims cannot be firmly matched to this ship.

**5. 11 Oct cluster**
- **Angola Prosperity:** the only one with a fast, timed UKMTO warning (164-26, strike at 1200Z).
- **Petro Dolphin:** UKMTO first issued a "suspicious activity" warning; Indian officials (Sonowal, the Indian embassy in Muscat) supplied the casualty details and name. The UKMTO reclassification to an attack came at 21:02Z.
- **Kition M, Ever Vict, Kaveri, Al Maqam:** UKMTO logged these as "time-late" reports (warnings 166–169), posted on 12 Oct, about 18:20Z for 167. In practice these four events reached the market more than 24 hours late.
- **Al Maqam:** the IRGC's Tasnim video appears to be the first public identification.
- **"Al Maqam = Liberian bulk carrier disabled" in the Wikipedia row:** this conflicts with JMIC, UKMTO and Seatrade, which all describe a VLCC tanker struck by a projectile. The "disabled bulk carrier" is most likely Ocean Molica: CENTCOM disabled it on 10 Oct, and CENTCOM's X post was at 00:06:54Z on 11 Oct [snowflake 2109073217176637447].
