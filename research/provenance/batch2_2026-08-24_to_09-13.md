# News-provenance study, batch 2 (24 Aug – 13 Sep 2026)

Method: WebSearch (standard and extended) plus WebFetch of the articles. Several primary pages (ukmto.org PDFs, Seatrade, CNBC, X) returned 403 or 402 errors, so some UKMTO details come from secondary quotes of the warning text.

**X/Twitter timestamps** were decoded from the post IDs using the snowflake formula `((id >> 22) + 1288834974657) ms`. These are exact UTC post times.

**"Live-blog entry" times** (CBS) are when the outlet posted the item. They are upper bounds on when the news was public, not the event time.

Times marked "(tz?)" are page timestamps whose time zone the page does not state. I made no timestamps up. If a time is missing, the table says so.

## Per-event notes

1. **Metro Venetian.** The sources disagree on time. Maritime Optima says "2025 local time". Defconlevel says UKMTO logged the report at 20:25 UTC. Gulf News gives 20:57 UTC (00:57 GST, 25 Aug). The most likely reading is that 2025Z is the report time and 2057Z is the warning time. The first social pickup I found was @sentdefender at 22:35:02Z, about 1.5–2 h after UKMTO. The vessel name came from Greek media (eKathimerini), not from UKMTO. MarEx reported a different date (25 Aug), which shows how easy it is to conflate this with event 2.
2. **Al Salam II.** This is the clearest case of a long reporting lag. The incident was at 17:30Z on 25 Aug, but UKMTO Warning 121-26 is dated 27 Aug. Industry chatter came about a day earlier: EOS Risk on the afternoon of 26 Aug, and MarEx at 23:08 on 26 Aug. A news-latency model keyed only to UKMTO warnings would have been about 36–60 h late here.
3. **Burgan.** This was also a delayed report (incident 20:53Z on 29 Aug, warning 30 Aug). The name came from JMIC and analysts. Iran's WANA (category b) carried the "ran aground" detail. Burgan's "abandoned" status, as given on Wikipedia, was not confirmed in anything I could open.
4. **Larak.** This is a military, not maritime, event, so the sources differ. Iranian agencies (Fars, Tasnim) reported blasts and a "drone attack". At the same time, wires (Reuters/AP/Axios) carried an anonymous US official, and CENTCOM followed on X. The IRGC's retaliation claim came through IRIB/IRNA/Fars "within hours". Jordan's interception statement came at dawn on 31 Aug. I found no clock times before the CBS live-blog stamp at 07:37Z on 31 Aug.
5. **31 Aug cluster.** The IRGC's mine claim (Iranian state TV) came out during the day, before the Sidr and Senegal Prosperity strikes (~19:52Z). It is therefore not a claim for those strikes; CENTCOM rebutted it the same day. The strikes followed the usual pattern: UKMTO at 20:00Z, then FX/commodity squawk sites and Iran International about 3 h later. Reuters named both ships the next morning (Asia time), using Marisks and Kpler. Bahri and the Saudi government (category c) came last, about 40 h after the strike, with the deaths and the attribution to Iran. Bahri's 23:40 local and Marisks' 19:52Z differ by about 50 minutes. I could not access the Windward note about VLCCs near Khasab.
6. **2 Sep.** This was claim-only (b). It was first visible in Iranian outlets on the morning of 2 Sep UTC. I found no UKMTO or JMIC incident to match it. It is probably a recycled reference to the 31 Aug strikes.
7. **5 Sep.** Iranian media were first, reporting blasts near Kharg about 6.5 h before CENTCOM's statement. CENTCOM's statement was the first to name the ships (Downy, Stark 1, Kylo). The IRGC's "3 tankers hit" counter-claim came about 6 h after CENTCOM and has no neutral confirmation.
8. **9 Sep.** For **Hercules Star**, UKMTO's warning (02:48Z) came about 9.5 h before the charterer's statement via Reuters, which was the first to name the ship (~12:20Z). For **New Andros**, UKMTO and the Iraqi port officials/Reuters (category c via d) were close together. OSINT accounts on X/Telegram supplied the name and video within hours. The "12:48 PM" and "2:04 PM" times on the pizzint feed have no stated time zone.
9. **El Gaia.** UKMTO (late 12 Sep / 13 Sep) came first. The IRGC's mine claim and CENTCOM's X post with the name and attribution both came about 1–1.5 days later; CENTCOM's was the first public naming. Oman's MSC (category c) gave the crew status on 15 Sep.

### Gaps and caveats
- I could not open ukmto.org PDFs (403), X posts (402), Seatrade, or the Windward insights page (truncated). The UKMTO warning DTGs come from secondary quotes.
- The CBS live-blog times are when CBS posted each item, not when the original source released it.
- Do not treat a time marked "(tz?)" as UTC.
