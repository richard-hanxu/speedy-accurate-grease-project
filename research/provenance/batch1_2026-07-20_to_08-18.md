# News provenance — batch 1 (20 Jul – 18 Aug 2026)

## How the times were established
- **UKMTO/JMIC primary docs**: I downloaded the JMIC Advisory Note updates 074–091 and two UKMTO Warning PDFs from ukmto.org. Their incident tables give UTC report times, and the PDF CreationDate gives the issue time. They're saved in `scratchpad/pdfs/`. Updates 083/084 (about 10–14 Aug) could not be found.
- **Article timestamps**: taken from each page's `datePublished` metadata (JSON-LD / `article:published_time`) and converted to UTC. Maritime Executive metadata is in US Eastern time (-04:00). The page body shows it without a zone, so it is easy to misread as UTC.
- **X/Twitter posts**: times were decoded from the post-ID snowflake: `(id >> 22) + 1288834974657` ms. This gives an exact UTC time.
- "≤" means "no later than". The source had already reported by the time the citing article appeared.
- UKMTO's own X posts could not be read directly. When the earliest item is "UKMTO", the time is the earliest article quoting it, so the true UKMTO post time is somewhat earlier.

## Per-event notes

1. **Kavomaleas / Acheloos / Kaifan (20 Jul).** These are three separate UKMTO serials:
   - 092-26: Kavomaleas, 0200 local, 8NM NW of Kumzar.
   - 093-26: 17NM E of Dibba, steering gear damaged. Possibly Acheloos, but this is unconfirmed.
   - 094-26: Kaifan, 6–8NM off Limah, distress about 20:10Z.
   The IRGC/Tasnim generic claim followed UKMTO within about an hour. Ship names came from the owner (Dynacom) and from private risk firms (Marisks, EOS Risk), about 14–20 h after the strike. The tow to Iran surfaced only about 3 days later, via satellite imagery and the FT. Note: a separate Tasnim claim dated 18 Jul 08:26Z refers to an earlier incident.
2. **Lavine (24 Jul).** This is a US-initiated event, so CENTCOM is the origin. UKMTO lagged by about a day ("third-party reports", 096-26). The ship's identity (Lavine = Disha) was later disputed by TankerTrackers and MarEx. The exact time of CENTCOM's first post was not visible; the earliest relay I could time is OSINTdefender at 21:59Z.
3. **GasLog Shanghai (31 Jul).** UKMTO named no ship. The name came about 12–17 h later, from GasLog itself and Bloomberg, and from Windward on 2 Aug. A near miss on a VLCC (102-26, 22:06Z) happened the same night. Windward links an IRGC claim, but that claim was published before this strike.
4. **Minoan Pioneer (3 Aug).** This is the clearest example of the UKMTO-first pattern: UKMTO 22:10Z, then the PDF 1.5 h later, then the first trade-press and wire items about 3–5 h later. Vanguard Tech supplied the name and the missing-crew detail within about 5 h. I found no source for the oil slick.
5. **Kumzar explosions (5 Aug).** Low-impact advisory. The roughly 6 h gap between event and wire pickup suggests UKMTO posted it late in the evening UTC.
6. **ADNOC tanker (8 Aug).** The Gulf government or state company was first. UKMTO's own record cites the ADNOC public statement as its source. The ship was never named publicly.
7. **Vela Nova (11 Aug).** Wikipedia's 12 Aug date is US-day and coverage lag; the strike was on 11 Aug. UKMTO first reported a "tanker", then corrected it to "container ship". Vanguard named the vessel, and CENTCOM acknowledged its role about 10 h after the UKMTO advisory.
8. **Navig8 Messi / Tarif (13 Aug).** The UAE MoFA statement was about 5 h after the strike and about 10 h *before* UKMTO even logged it. The ship names came from Seatrade the next morning.
9. **Al Watan (14/15 Aug).** UKMTO's DTG implies the strike was at 20:00Z 14 Aug. Lloyd's List says 2000 UTC on 15 Aug; Wikipedia gives 14 Aug. Do not confuse it with UKMTO 116-26, a separate bulk carrier hit at 22:00Z 15 Aug with a casualty, which UKMTO received late and issued only on 18 Aug at 14:01Z. Gulf News conflates the two.
10. **Minoan Dignity (17–18 Aug).** The vessel was dark (no contact) for about 7 h after the SSAS alerts. The first public report was Reuters citing UKMTO, about 8.5 h after the CSO alert. The name and fatality came from Greek media, Vanguard and INTERCARGO, about 7–13 h after that. This is the sister ship of Minoan Pioneer (same manager, Modion).

## Caveats
- I could not read UKMTO's own X/website warning times for most events (the site blocks scraping, and only 104-26 and 116-26 PDFs were retrievable). "UKMTO public ≤X" is therefore an upper bound taken from the first article citing it.
- Bloomberg, Reuters and Lloyd's List originals are mostly paywalled or have no timestamps. Their times come from syndicated copies (US News, BOE Report, Ground News).
- Times on Telegram, Tasnim and Fars could not be checked directly, except through the Oman Observer citation in event 1.
